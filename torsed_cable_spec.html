A muscle made of phase.

This is an interactive design specification for a torsed cable tube system — a synthetic muscle architecture where four independently controlled cable layers produce coordinated gesture through phase-offset motor signals.

Each layer has a distinct mechanical role, motor type, and biological analog. Click any layer on the left to begin exploring.

Layer 4
Outer braid
torsional containment · slow sweep

The outer braid is the fascia of this system — the containing membrane that gives all inner phases something to tension against. It runs at the slowest sweep frequency of the four layers, holding structural coherence across the full length of the tube.

Without this layer, faster inner coil torsion would have no frame. The braid is what converts inner phase events into directed force rather than dissipated rotation.

Layer 3
Intermediate ring
phase transfer · coupling layer

The intermediate ring is the tendon junction of the system. It neither generates primary force nor contains the structure — it transfers phase information between the fast inner coil and the slow outer braid.

Its motor runs at a medium cadence, deliberately out of phase with both adjacent layers. This differential is the coupling mechanism — the ring mediates the speed mismatch and prevents destructive interference between layer 2's bursts and layer 4's hold.

Layer 2
Inner coil
actuation layer · fast twitch response

The inner coil is where the gesture actually lives. The brushless motor rotates a field; the wound cable geometry converts that rotation into axial shortening — torsion becoming contraction. Three piezo fiber nodes cascade along the coil length, each translating the mechanical pressure event into a sub-micron dimensional change.

This is the fast twitch layer. High pole count motor, high frequency, short burst. It fires against the frame provided by layer 3 and layer 4. The output is not continuous force — it is a discrete, quantized shortening event, exactly like a sarcomere stepping 4nm per calcium gate.

Layer 1
Structural core
axial load · postural hold

The structural core does not move. It holds. Under continuous low motor tension it maintains the axial geometry of the entire tube — providing the fixed reference point against which all three outer layers generate their phase differentials.

Biologically this is the slow twitch postural layer — the muscle that keeps you upright for eight hours without conscious effort. It barely fires. Its signal is almost DC. What it provides is not force but reference — the zero against which the gesture is measured.

System
Signal controller
4-channel variable phase · nervous system

The signal controller is the nervous system. It sends four independent phase signals — one to each layer — at different frequencies and with programmable phase offsets between them.

The gesture the tube produces is not a property of any single layer. It is the interference pattern of all four phase signals operating simultaneously against each other. Slow the outer braid and speed the inner coil: the tube contracts. Offset layer 3 by 90°: the tube twists. Run all four in synchrony: maximum axial force. This is how biology produces infinite gesture from a finite muscle inventory — not by switching muscles on and off, but by modulating their relative phase.
